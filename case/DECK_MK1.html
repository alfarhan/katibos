INPUTS

Three objects now, not two

The display no longer arrives as a bare PCB. It arrives as a finished brick — board, cell and a snap-fit case that already solved the window, the cutouts and the cell retention. So the enclosure's job shrank: it does not house a board any more, it docks a brick.

I could not read your case's model page — printables.com is blocked from here, so every figure below that depends on its geometry is written as an expression in T, its outer thickness, rather than a number I made up. One caliper measurement resolves the whole sheet set. Sheet A7 tabulates the outcomes.

A1
DRIVERS

What having the case already changes

  1. Nothing crosses the hinge. At all. The cell is in the brick, the keyboard is BLE. Previous revisions spent a whole sheet deciding where the 18650 goes and what it costs to route two wires through a knuckle — that question is now closed, and with it a dummy cell, a hatch, a connector and three printed parts.
  2. The lid is a frame, not a housing. No board pocket, no M2.5 bosses, no back cover, no I/O window to cut — the case did all of that. lid_centre becomes a 4 mm plate with a hole in it, which is why the part count fell from eleven to eight.
  3. Both halves stay removable. The keyboard lifts out and goes back to the desk; the brick pops out of the cradle and is a standalone reader again. Nothing in this build is captive, which is unusual for a writerdeck and worth keeping on purpose.
  4. The brick is thicker than a purpose-built island would be, and that cost is real: a bare board in a moulded pocket shuts at 46, the docked brick shuts at 28 + T. Sheet A7 says what that buys and how to get the millimetres back if you want them.
  5. The cradle grips only two edges. Open on the left and right, so it does not matter which edge carries USB-C — a question that cost me a checklist item in every earlier revision.
  6. 200 mm part cap, from rev.8. Every part fits a 220 × 220 bed.
A2
SHUT / OPEN

The two states, on the centreline

Shut, the brick fills the lid plate and protrudes T − 4 from its outer face; over the rest of the lid the slab is 32 mm. Open, the lid stands at 105° from the base and puts the glass centre 84 mm above the desk. Both sections are drawn at T = 28 as a placeholder.

A3
PLANS

Base and lid, and where they split

Both halves split into a 200 mm centre part and two 54 mm caps. The cradle window sits wholly inside the lid's centre part, and every joint lands under the keyboard or under a flat flank.

A4
CENTRING

Centre the picture, not the case

Waveshare's active area is not centred on its PCB: it sits 1.50 mm from the left edge and 6.20 mm from the right, where the ribbon folds. Whoever drew your snap case either compensated for that or didn't, and you inherit whichever they chose. Measure the two bezel borders and shift the cradle by half the difference — then the image is centred on the lid whatever the case does.

The lip earns its place twice. A 2.1 mm rim around the window on the lid's inner face keeps the keycaps off the glass when shut — and at 15° of rake it is also the small hood that rev.8 puts round its own screen, which at that angle shades nothing. Protection and no penalty.

A5
HINGE

Hinge — three plain groups, and now it needs real torque

A 308 mm barrel would need a 308 mm pin, which does not exist off the shelf. Three groups instead: two 60 mm near the ends and one 40 mm in the middle, on M3 × 70 and M3 × 50 pins — the same screws rev.8's own BOM already calls for. None of them is hollow any more: nothing passes through.

Buy the torque hinges. The brick is board + case + cell, call it ~165 g, on a ~150 g lid plate: roughly 2 kgf·cm at the pin. Printed knuckle friction held the 150 g lid of the previous revision; it will creep under this one. A pair of 3–4 kgf·cm mini torque hinges pocketed into the outer groups is the fix, and it is the only part of this design I would not try to print.

Hardware

  • 8 × M3 × 16 socket cap + 8 × M3 heat-set inserts — two base joints, two lid joints
  • 2 × mini torque hinges, 3–4 kgf·cm — or 2 × M3 × 70 + 1 × M3 × 50 with PTFE washers if you want to try printed friction first
  • 4 × Ø6 × 3 neodymium magnets — 2 in the lid's front edge, 2 in the base's front shelf
  • 4 × Ø12 silicone feet + 4 pads in the keyboard pocket

No M2.5 screws, no connector, no glue. The display brick is never opened.

Print notes

PETG or ASA, not PLA — a 300 mm span that has softened in a car is scrap. 0.2 mm layers, 3 walls, 15% gyroid; 4 walls on the knuckles and on lid_centre, which at 4 mm over 308 mm is the part that will flex. Estimated ~265 cm³ of modelled solid, so roughly 300 g of filament — against 842 cm³ for rev.8's twenty parts.

A7
ONE NUMBER

Shut height is 28 + T. Everything else is decided.

28 mm is the base rim plus the lid rim; T is your case's outer thickness. Measure it across the thickest point — over the cell, not over the bezel — and read off the row.

Case thickness TShut heightVerdict
1644Thinner than a purpose-built island. Take it.
2048Level with a purpose-built island. No reason to reprint anything.
2553Fine. Still 7 mm under rev.8.
2856Acceptable. This is the figure the drawings assume.
3058Getting fat. Read the two escapes below.
32+60+You have lost the whole advantage over rev.8. Take an escape.

Two escapes, if your number is at the bottom of that table

  • Merge the case into the lid. Take the snap case's own geometry and build it into lid_centre as a moulded pocket: one part instead of two, no cradle rim, no tabs, and the plate thickness stops being wasted — back to ~46 mm shut. The cost is a case that already works and already fits, and the display stops being a standalone device.
  • Fold the screen backwards instead of forwards. Hinge at the front of a rear well and let the brick lie face-down in a 100 × 78 × T pocket behind the keyboard, with a separate thin cover over the keys. Shut height becomes max(28, T + 6) — about 34 mm — because the brick no longer stacks on top of the base. It costs ~70 mm of depth (base goes to ~200) and two lids instead of one. It is the thinnest slab available with this brick, and if the number in that table horrifies you, this is the design worth drawing next.

What docking buys, to weigh against those millimetres: the board is never disassembled, the ports and buttons are already cut and always reachable, the cell retention is already solved and proven, and the display remains a device in its own right — which is the same property the Air60 has. Both halves of this writerdeck can walk away and be useful alone. That is worth several millimetres.

Where rev.8 is still ahead

  • The screen sits higher — its lid is 133 long against our 122, and it is not carrying a 74 mm-tall brick that has to fit inside the lid's length. 84 mm is a typewriter's eye line, which is defensible, but it is lower.
  • It owns its keyboard. Switches, caps and layout are yours, and no BLE pairing can go wrong.
  • It has been built. Nothing here has been printed, and the hinge in particular is drawn, not proven.
A9
BEFORE CAD

Four measurements, and the sheet set is real

The list used to have seven items. Owning the case retired three of them and the battery retired a fourth.

  1. 01T — the case's outer thickness at its thickest point. Over the cell, not the bezel. Sets the shut height directly and nothing else competes for importance.
  2. 02The case's outer footprint, X × Y, plus whether its edges are square or drafted. Sets the cradle window and how the sprung tabs have to reach.
  3. 03The bezel borders left and right of the glass. Cradle offset = (R − L) / 2 — sheet A4. Takes ten seconds and is invisible if you skip it and wrong.
  4. 04Your keyboard's length, depth and rear height with keycaps on. Spec says 297.3 × 107.2 and 22.0; the base rim is 28 and the shut clearance over the lip is 2.1. A 2 mm surprise means the lid rests on your keys.

Nothing here touches the firmware. The waveshare env already drives this panel, hosts BLE keyboards and reads the cell on GPIO4 — and since the cell never leaves the brick, that reading stays exactly as it is. Worth knowing about Sleep: it is hidden on this board because a BLE keyboard cannot wake a radio that is off, and shutting the lid changes nothing about that. Closing this device is not a power state; it is just a lid.

Send me T, the footprint and the two borders and this becomes a dimensioned set rather than a parametric one — and if T lands past 30, say so and I will draw the fold-backwards variant from sheet A7 instead.
